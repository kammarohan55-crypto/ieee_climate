# TerraTrace project walkthrough

This is a hands-on project guide. The hosted website remains offline. A temporary loopback preview requires the explicit `TERRATRACE_LOCAL_PREVIEW=1` flag; see the PowerShell instructions in [README](../README.md). Leave that flag unset in production. Source code: [https://github.com/kammarohan55-crypto/ieee_climate](https://github.com/kammarohan55-crypto/ieee_climate). Hackathon submission remains deferred; no video is produced.

1. **Open the city twin.** Choose a featured city. Check the weather source time and map capture date. The mapped district is a bounded sample, not the complete city.
2. **Inspect the district.** Expand the map, pan and zoom, switch between plan and isometric views, and search for a building. Inspect its footprint, source tags and height provenance.
3. **Explore an intervention.** Change roof treatments and proposed trees on the one large map. Keep assumed demand, grid factor, performance and transferred cooling assumptions visible. Read energy and temperature comparisons as hypothetical scenarios.
4. **Inspect roof surveys and shade.** The survey ranks building capacity under editable usable-area assumptions. The animated shade timeline illustrates ground shadows; it does not change PV output or establish observed thermal comfort.
5. **Save and compare strategies.** Name the working plan and save up to four alternatives. Select a Reference plan and a Displayed plan. The entire map, inspector, main outcomes and hourly chart show the Displayed plan; the tradeoff plot and exact table compare quantities. All alternatives share one frozen forecast, map and reference assumptions. Use Recalculate all to adopt current data for every plan together, or Apply to draft to return an alternative to the working plan. Export or seal the full comparison.
6. **Plan the site survey.** Open Evidence and compare height-source categories. Inspect a footprint-density cell or search for a building by name, use or OSM ID. Add it to the survey list and export the checklist. Missing tags remain survey questions; a saved entry is not a completed survey.
7. **Follow roof water into a tank.** In the separate Rainwater lab, use a building selected on the shared map or one of the four largest mapped catchments in the survey queue. Play or scrub tomorrow's 24-hour forecast. The diagram follows real liquid rain through connected runoff, storage, non-potable reuse and overflow. The chart keeps rain in mm and storage in m³; expand the hourly table for exact balances. Edit tank capacity, starting fill, connected roof share, runoff coefficient and assumed demand. A dry forecast can reuse only assumed starting water.
8. **Compare capacity and prepare a survey.** The fixed options are 0, 1, 2.5, 5, 10, 25, 50 and 100 m³, plus a distinct custom capacity. Every comparison starts empty and shares the same roof, rain and demand. Selecting an option resets the main scenario's starting fill to zero. These one-day results are not annual tank recommendations or flooding predictions. Mark the roof, gutter/first-flush, non-potable demand/treatment and tank-location checks manually. Export or seal the weather, map source, assumptions, 24-hour balances, candidate results and checklist. Checks apply to the selected scenario; a map footprint does not establish a safe collection system.
9. **Read the research and preserve evidence.** The library links 11 papers and 10 organizer-verified winning projects, reviewed on 8 October 2026. Each explains its design contribution and transfer limits. Seal a city scenario, water plan, strategy comparison or environmental snapshot, export it, then import it into the independent verifier. Changing a committed value causes verification to fail. Previously exported receipts remain supported. Optional Sepolia anchoring requires a deployed registry and wallet confirmation; no public anchor is bundled.
10. **Review the remaining roadmap.** Measured-change and later shaded-route tools remain proposed. The water lab is implemented, but measured demand, rainfall observations, pipe and tank surveys, long-term sizing and drainage validation are still needed for an operational pilot. Source code is available in the linked GitHub repository; video and hackathon submission remain deferred.
